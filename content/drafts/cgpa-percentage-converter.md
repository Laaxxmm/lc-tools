### 2. Why does VTU subtract 0.75 before multiplying?

The tool page already answers this:

> It is a fixed offset written into the VTU regulation, not something you derive. VTU sets percentage as (CGPA minus 0.75) times 10, which is roughly 7.5 marks below what a plain 10x conversion would give you. VTU does not publish a justification for the number and you do not need one. What matters is that using 10x instead inflates your percentage and can make an application form disagree with your degree certificate.

### 3. Is 9.5 a UGC rule or a CBSE rule?

The tool page already answers this:

> It started with CBSE. CBSE used a 9.5 multiplier to convert its own Class 10 grade point average into an indicative percentage, and a number of universities and employers borrowed the same figure afterwards. It is not a national standard, and it is not a UGC instruction that binds your university. Use 9.5 only if your university prints 9.5.

### 4. Will CAT, MAT or PGCET accept the percentage this tool gives me?

### 6. My CGPA is out of 4, or out of 8. Can I use this?

The tool page already answers this:

> Not directly. Every formula here assumes a 10-point scale, so a 3.6 on a 4-point scale will be read as 3.6 out of 10 and the answer will be badly wrong. The custom multiplier gets you an approximation if your institution has given you one, but a four-point GPA usually needs a formal equivalence letter rather than arithmetic.
